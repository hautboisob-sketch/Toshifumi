# Overseas (non-Japan) Competitor Landscape: AI-Generated Apparel Design × Print-on-Demand × Creator Marketplaces (as of Oct 2026)

> Research method note: These notes were compiled on 2026-10-15. Direct page fetching was blocked for most target domains in this environment (DNS/proxy refusals for printful.com, printify.com, redbubble.com, zazzle.com, techcrunch.com), and the session's web-search quota ran out before every lead could be followed up. ## Q1. Which POD / apparel platforms have built-in AI design generators (2025–2026), and what are the specifics?

### Takeaway
Basic AI image generation is now a free, rate-limited standard feature at the large POD fulfillers. Printful and Printify (merged in 2024) each give 15 free generations per day. The biggest new entrant is Amazon: since June 8, 2026, any U.S. shopper can prompt a design through Alexa for Shopping and order it printed via Merch on Demand at no design cost. Printify's ChatGPT app (April 2026) already runs on Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash Image ("Nano Banana"), which is the same model family the Japanese plan intends to use. The marketplaces (Redbubble, TeePublic, Threadless, Society6) have no built-in generator that I could find. They act as sales channels for AI art made elsewhere.

### Cited Findings

**Printful (Design Maker + AI Image Generator)**
- Printful's AI Image Generator sits inside its Design Maker. Each Printful account gets **15 free image-generation attempts per day**. Each attempt makes one image, and the limit resets every 24 hours. The flow is: pick a product → Start designing → AI option → text prompt → choose a visual style (or "None") → generate. — [Printful Help Center](https://support.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/41396543812625)
- The Design Maker also offers file upload, a text tool, a graphics library and a referral to a Fiverr designer. — [Printful Help: Design Maker](https://support.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/41396532234129); [Printful Help: creating images](https://support.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/41396495413393)
- Printful's documentation doesn't name the model behind the generator, and a third-party review notes "very little information" on Printful's site about the tool. — [ecommerce-platforms.com review](https://ecommerce-platforms.com/?p=97059)
- Third-party Shopify app "CAI Customizer for Printful" adds AI customization on top of Printful. — [Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/cai-customizer)

**Printify (AI Image Generator + ChatGPT app)**
- Printify's AI Image Generator is free. Its blog says the tool was upgraded and has been **powered by OpenAI since August 2024**. — [Printify blog](https://printify.com/blog/ai-image-generator/); [Printify AI Image Generator page](https://printify.com/ai-image-generator/)
- Every registered merchant gets **15 free attempts per day**. Each attempt produces one **1024×1024** image, which Printify auto-upscales when the order goes to production. Other features: style modifiers (e.g., Pop Art, Minimalism), prompt enhancement and optional background removal. — [Printify Help Center](https://help.printify.com/hc/en-us/articles/15493218417809-How-can-I-design-a-product-using-Printify-s-AI-Image-Generator)
- Third-party reviews disagree on the number of styles (11 during beta vs. 13 later). — [ecommerce-platforms.com](https://ecommerce-platforms.com/?p=97059); [project-aeon.com](https://project-aeon.com/blogs/ai-shirt-design-generator)
- **Printify ChatGPT app (launched ~April 2026):** a user describes a gift idea, the app generates a print-ready design, applies it to a product and guides checkout. Payment happens on a Printify-hosted page outside ChatGPT. The app is free and needs no Printify account. Products are limited to **T-shirts, hoodies and tote bags**. Only U.S. users can use it, but orders can ship to other regions. — [Printify Help Center](https://help.printify.com/hc/en-us/articles/43090996796689-How-do-I-order-personalized-gifts-through-the-new-Printify-App-in-ChatGPT); [Printify blog, Apr 21, 2026](https://printify.com/blog/chatgpt-gift-ideas/); [Press release](https://news.marketersmedia.com/printify-launches-chatgpt-app-to-power-personalized-gifting-through-ai/89191158)
- The app's terms say user inputs are sent to **Google Gemini image generation models**, and the FAQ names the generator as **"NanoBanana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image Generator)"**. — [Printify ChatGPT App Terms of Use](https://printify.com/chatgpt-app-terms-of-use/); [Printify ChatGPT landing page](https://printify.com/printify_chatgpt/)
- Printify says the app won silver (Business Innovation) at the Merit Awards. This is a company claim. — [Printify LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/printify)

**Printful + Printify merger (context)**
- The merger was announced Nov 5, 2024 as a merger of equals and completed after regulatory approval. Integration began Nov 20, 2024. Alex Saltonstall (ex-Printful CEO) is CEO and Anastasija Oleinika (ex-Printify CEO) is President/Head of Platform. Both brands keep operating separately under a holding company. — [Printful newsroom](https://www.printful.com/ca/news/merged+company+leadership+team); [ASI Central, Nov 2024](https://members.asicentral.com/news/industry-news/november-2024/e-commerce-platforms-printful-printify-merge/)
- Latvian public broadcaster LSM reported in Jan 2025 that the holding company is named **"Fyul"** and that the merger aims to prepare for a future stock-market listing. This is a single source. — [LSM, Jan 8, 2025](https://eng.lsm.lv/article/economy/business/08.01.2025-merger-of-the-year-as-printful-and-printify-become-fyul.a582814/)

**Amazon (Alexa for Shopping AI merch + Merch on Demand), June 2026**
- On **June 8, 2026**, Amazon added a feature to Alexa for Shopping (Shopping app and Amazon.com) that turns text prompts into merch designs. Shoppers refine designs by typing changes or tapping suggestions, and Merch on Demand handles printing and Prime-eligible shipping. The feature is **U.S.-only** and **free**; customers pay only for products. — [TechCrunch, Jun 8, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/amazon-now-lets-you-design-custom-merch-using-ai/); [About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/design-merch-with-ai-alexa-for-shopping); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/amazon/2026/amazon-deploys-alexa-ai-to-disrupt-print-on-demand-market)

Alexa for Shopping itself launched in mid-May 2026, merging Rufus and Alexa+. — [Digital Commerce 360, Jun 8, 2026](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/06/08/amazons-alexa-for-shopping-adds-customization-feature-for-merch/); [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/amazon-adds-an-ai-tool-to-alexa-for-shopping-that-turns-text-prompts-into-custom-print-on-demand-merch-designs/)

**AI-asset marketplace precedent**
- **Creative Fabrica** (marketplace for POD assets, fonts and graphics) launched the generative tool **CF Spark** in Oct 2022. Creators can sell AI-generated files such as T-shirt designs there. — [TechCrunch, Oct 31, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/31/digital-assets-marketplace-creative-fabrica-launches-generative-ai-tool/)

### Inferences
- AI generation itself is commoditized. Printful and Printify give 15 free generations per day, and Amazon's consumer generator is free. A Japanese entrant can't charge for "AI design" alone. Its revenue has to come from the printed product margin and/or the marketplace take rate.
- Printify picking Gemini 2.5 Flash Image for its consumer ChatGPT flow, after using OpenAI in its merchant tool, shows that Google's image models are already in production use for exactly this use case. This supports the Japanese plan's model choice and also shows that rivals can easily switch models.
- Amazon's June 2026 launch is the closest analogue to the "design with AI → order" half of the Japanese concept. It deliberately leaves out a creator marketplace and royalty layer, so that layer is a point of differentiation.

### Gaps
- I couldn't confirm the model behind Printful's generator, whether Printify's in-dashboard generator has also moved to Gemini, or which model Amazon's merch generator uses (pages not fetchable; search quota exhausted).
- Zazzle's, Spreadshirt's and Teemill's AI generator specifics (model, limits, launch date) are unverified. I found no Teemill AI generator at all.
- There is no official confirmation of AI design features at Spring, Bonfire, Merch by Amazon (seller side), Placeit or Custom Ink. Placeit was not researched because of the quota.
- Canva Print's T-shirt availability and any AI-to-print integration were not confirmed.

## Q2. Which startups are specifically "AI T-shirt designer + order (+ marketplace)"? Funding, traction, status.

### Takeaway
Many small consumer sites now let you prompt a design and order a printed shirt: GPTShirt.ai, Shirrts, ShirtGen.ai, Sizzlepop and YupTees. None that I found also runs a user-to-user marketplace with royalties, and none reports venture funding or traction figures. Funded AI-fashion startups mostly target brands and designers (Raspberry AI, MYTH, AI.Fashion) or try-on (Doji), not consumer POD. Among the creator-commerce incumbents, Spring (ex-Teespring) is winding down its legacy platform under Amaze. Articore's new creator-storefront brand Dashery is growing from a small base.

### Cited Findings

**Consumer "prompt → design → order printed shirt" sites (funding not disclosed)**
- **GPTShirt.ai:** type a prompt or upload an image, preview on a real garment, then order a DTG print with no minimum. — [GPTShirt.ai](https://gptshirt.ai/); [GPTShirt custom tees](https://gptshirt.ai/custom-t-shirts)
- **Shirrts:** turns a written description into a finished T-shirt design, then prints and ships it. — [Shirrts](https://www.shirrts.com/)
- **ShirtGen.ai:** free trial with 4 designs; "premium eco-friendly inks"; advertises 3–5 business-day delivery. — [ShirtGen.ai](https://shirtgen.ai/en)
- **Sizzlepop:** free generator with no signup, one design per generation and occasional capacity limits. Shirts are printed in the USA after the customer approves a proof, or the file can be downloaded. — [Sizzlepop](https://sizzlepop.ai/t-shirt-generator/)
- **YupTees** (press release, Sept 2026): free generator with no sign-up; outputs 4500×5400 files with and without transparent backgrounds and a commercial-use license per design. It targets POD sellers. — [Press release](https://news.marketersmedia.com/yuptees-launches-free-ai-t-shirt-design-generator-for-print-on-demand-sellers-no-sign-up-required/89202151)
- **Tshirtdesignai.com:** design tool only (no printing found); $19/month Pro tier; exports 300 DPI transparent PNGs. — [TshirtDesignAI](https://tshirtdesignai.com/)
- Other generator-only tools often listed for POD include Morphic (free plan watermarked), Krea (commercial use needs a paid plan), insMind, WeShop, Fotor, StarryAI and NightCafe. — [Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/tools/ai-t-shirt-design-generator); [Krea](https://canvas.krea.ai/apps/image/t-shirt-design-maker); [insMind](https://www.insmind.com/design/ai-t-shirt-design-generator); [StarryAI](https://starryai.com/en/pages/ai-shirt-design-generator); [NightCafe](https://creator.nightcafe.studio/trend/ai-t-shirt-design-generator)

### Inferences
- The "AI design + order" consumer niche is crowded with thin, undifferentiated sites that appear unfunded. The combination of AI design, ordering and a royalty marketplace looks unoccupied by a dedicated startup, but incumbents could add the missing layer: Amazon already has Merch royalties, Printify already has storefronts, and Zazzle already has customizable royalty products.
- Spring's wind-down and Amaze's move to subscriptions suggest that pure creator-POD on thin margins struggled as a standalone business. This is consistent with Articore's need to raise artist fees to return to profit (see Q3 and Q5).

### Gaps
- The user-named examples "Shirtfusion" and "Creati" could not be identified as real AI-apparel companies. creati.ai appears to be an AI-tool directory. "Teemill AI" was not found.
- None of the consumer AI-shirt sites disclose funding, order volumes or traffic.
- I couldn't independently confirm the exact Spring shutdown date (Sept 15, 2026).
- Search quota ran out before I could check Placeit (Envato) AI, Picsart print integrations, Vistaprint AI, and NightCafe/Playground print shops.

## Q3. Royalty / revenue-share structures of creator marketplaces (concrete numbers)

### Takeaway
Creator earnings are being squeezed across the board. Redbubble takes a 50% (Standard) or 20% (Premium) platform fee plus a 50% fee on markup above 20%. Amazon Merch's June 2026 tiers roughly halve royalties on organic-traffic sales (about $2.44 on a $19.99 tee). Threadless moved its marketplace to a flat 10% royalty. Society6 reportedly fixes markups at 5–10%. Zazzle's default is a 5% royalty (up to 50%) minus marketing-royalty fees. TeePublic pays fixed amounts of roughly $4 per tee at full price and $2 on sale, per third-party sources. Typical real-world creator take per shirt is about $1–5.

### Cited Findings

**Redbubble (Articore)**
- Platform fee on monthly gross earnings: **Standard 50%, Premium 20%, Pro 0%**. Total account fees are capped at **$150 / €150 / £150 per pay period**. — [Redbubble Help: platform fee](https://help.redbubble.com/hc/en-us/articles/50959494599060-How-does-the-platform-fee-get-calculated); [Redbubble Help](https://help.redbubble.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412593541908)
- **Excess Markup Fee** (from **Sept 1, 2025**): applies to Standard and Premium accounts, only to the portion of markup above 20%. Artists keep 50% of the extra-markup earnings, and Pro accounts are exempt. — [Redbubble blog, Aug 2025](https://blog.redbubble.com/2025/08/excess-markup-fee-explained/)
- Artist pay = base price + markup set by the artist. Illustrators Australia notes that for small sellers on the default 20% markup, the tier fees can whittle the effective margin down to about 10%. — [Redbubble Help: payment calculation](https://help.redbubble.com/hc/en-us/articles/202270799); [Illustrators Australia](https://www.illustratorsaustralia.com/news/fears-for-tiers-at-redbubble/)
- One r/redbubble user asked "how on earth are these fees MORE than 50% of my income?" Payhip calls the fees expensive. — [Payhip](https://payhip.com/hub/what-is-redbubble/); [r/redbubble mirror](https://redlib.belloworld.it/r/redbubble)
- The payout threshold is either $20 or $10 (a reported drop from July 2026), depending on the source. — [Printify blog (says $20)](https://printify.com/blog/redbubble-vs-society6/) vs. [AutoKeyworder (says $10 from Jul 2026)](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/how-to-sell-on-redbubble)

**TeePublic (Articore)** — official royalty page not retrieved; third-party figures only
- About **$4 per T-shirt at the regular $20 price and $2 at the $13 sale price**. One 2026 blog says newer accounts get about $3 / $1, versus $4 / $2 for "Artisan" accounts. Hoodies pay about $8 and stickers $0.20–1.00. Because site-wide sales run almost daily, average payout per shirt is closer to $2. — [AutoKeyworder](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/sell-on-teepublic); [TopBubbleIndex](https://www.topbubbleindex.com/blog/?p=3699); [AutoKeyworder: TeePublic vs Redbubble](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/teepublic-vs-redbubble)
- Another source describes a 10% / 20% percentage model that conflicts with the fixed-amount model. It looks unreliable. — [brian.dev.libraryc.org](https://brian.dev.libraryc.org/?p=6330)

**Threadless**
- Artist Shops: **Artist earnings = retail price − base cost**, where base cost = product cost + a **5% processing fee** on the retail price. Payouts go through PayPal. — [Threadless Artist Shops Help](https://artistshopshelp.threadless.com/article/665-how-do-artist-earnings-work)
- From **Feb 3, 2025**, all Threadless Marketplace sales pay a **flat 10% royalty**. — [Threadless Support](https://support.threadless.com/article/171-how-do-artist-earnings-work)
- An older Threadless blog example: a $25 tee puts **$15 (60%)** in the artist's pocket in an Artist Shop (undated; base costs since changed). — [Threadless Creative Resources](https://creativeresources.threadless.com/artist-shops-vs-redbubble-vs-society6/)

**Zazzle**
- Creators set their own royalty ("Name Your Royalty"). The **standard royalty is 5%**, with a **maximum of 50% for physical products** and 99% for digital products. "Zazzle Heart" products and Value phone cases are fixed at 5%. — [Zazzle Help: Ambassador referral commissions & creator royalties](https://www.zazzle.co.nz/hc/article/ambassador_referral_commissions_creator_royalties-30810357489175)
- Zazzle also charges a **Marketing Royalty Fee** of **35% (Clothing & Shoes) to 50% (Invitations & Stationery, Weddings)** of gross royalty. Referral commissions are 35–50% for self-promotion and 15% for cross-promotion (effective Apr 1, 2025), and "Excess Royalty Fees" are deducted as applicable. — [same Zazzle Help page](https://www.zazzle.co.nz/hc/article/ambassador_referral_commissions_creator_royalties-30810357489175)

**Merch by Amazon / Merch on Demand**
- Royalty = offer price − applicable tax − Amazon's costs. — [Amazon Merch developer page](https://developer.amazon.com/zh/merch)
- From **June 1, 2026**, flat royalties are replaced by three traffic-source tiers. On a $19.99 tee:
  - **Creator** (default, mainly Amazon-organic traffic): about **$2.44**.
  - **Plus** (≥15% of U.S. unit sales from non-organic traffic such as Sponsored Ads or off-Amazon ads, and ≥10 sales/month): about **$4.88**.
  - **Premium** (~≥35% external traffic): about **$5.27**.
  - The previous flat royalty was about $4.88, so organic-only sellers see about a 50% cut.
  - These figures come from seller blogs; Amazon's own announcement was not retrieved. — [Merch Titans](https://merchtitans.com/blog/amazon-merch-royalty-changes-june-2026); [AMZPrep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-merch-on-demand/); [SellerSprite](https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/amazon-merch-royalties-and-pricing); [Gelato (NL)](https://www.gelato.com/nl/blog/amazon-print-on-demand)
- Precedent: Merch also cut royalties effective Jan 15, 2018. — [EcommerceBytes (Amazon Merch tag)](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/tag/amazon-merch)

**Spreadshirt / Spreadshop**
- Partners set a **design price (up to $15)** and always receive it, whichever channel the sale comes through. Spreadshop historically added a **20% affiliate commission** plus volume commissions (0.5%–40% under the older model). A later post says the affiliate commission no longer exists under a new commission model. — [Spreadshop blog FAQ](https://www.spreadshop.com/blog/?p=4938); [Spreadshop: why the commission model changed](https://www.spreadshop.com/blog/?p=20540); [Spreadshop: affiliate commission Q&A (2016)](https://www.spreadshop.com/blog/2016/03/16/qa-session-the-affiliate-commission/)
- In 2017 Spreadshirt claimed earnings of up to 60% of product price (20% affiliate + 40% volume) plus the design price. This is dated. — [EcommerceBytes, Feb 2017](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2017/02/27/making-million-dollars-selling-tshirts-online/)

**Society6**
- After its 2025 shift to a curated model, artists reportedly can no longer set prices, and markups are fixed at **5–10%** by product. This is a single third-party source. — [EcommerceCEO](https://www.ecommerceceo.com/best-society6-alternatives/)
- An older Printful comparison gives a 10% default margin adjustable up to 999%. — [Printful blog](https://www.printful.com/ca/blog/comparing-artist-profit-on-society6-vs-printful)

**Dashery (Articore)**: no account fees and no payout minimum. — [Dashery](https://dashery.com/)

### Inferences
- The structures fall into three patterns:
  - **Artist sets the margin, platform takes a fee:** Redbubble, Zazzle and Threadless Artist Shops.
  - **Platform fixes the royalty:** TeePublic, Threadless Marketplace at 10%, Society6 post-2025, and Amazon Merch tiers.
  - **Creator storefront where the creator keeps retail minus base cost:** Threadless Artist Shops, Dashery, Fourthwall, Spring and Printful/Printify stores.
- For a Japanese service, a royalty of roughly 10% of retail, or a fixed ¥300–800 per tee (mirroring the $2–5 per-shirt reality), would be in line with global norms. This is an inference, not a source.
- Two moves raised platform margins: Amazon tying higher royalties to creators bringing *external* traffic, and Redbubble cutting fees for "Pro" accounts. Both reward creators who market their own designs, which is a useful model for incentivizing user-driven promotion.

### Gaps
- Official TeePublic royalty amounts and the official Amazon June 2026 tier announcement were not retrieved.
- I didn't find the current Spreadshirt Marketplace commission percentage or the Bonfire, Spring and Fourthwall fee structures.
- Printful/Printify base costs for a standard tee (needed for margin modeling) weren't gathered because of the quota.

## Q4. AI-generated content policies, IP takedowns, moderation; notable incidents

### Takeaway
No major POD marketplace bans AI art outright. Policies range from Etsy's mandatory disclosure, through Society6's curation that cut AI and low-quality content, to Redbubble and TeePublic, which publish little explicit AI rule-text. Crowdspring is an outright ban. IP risk is the dominant issue. Courts have so far largely shielded Redbubble as a platform (Atari), but not completely (Brandy Melville). Algorithmic copying claims (Shein RICO case) and studio suits against image generators (Disney/Universal v. Midjourney) show that both AI generators and POD platforms are litigation targets. Automated "art-stealing" bots on Twitter were exposed in 2019.

### Cited Findings

**Platform AI policies**
- **Etsy:** under its 2024 Creativity Standards, every item is labeled made, designed, sourced or handpicked by the seller. "Designed by a seller" explicitly covers **seller-prompted AI art** that a production partner prints, and full disclosure is required. — [Printful Help: Etsy Creativity Standards](https://support.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/41396445789841-What-should-I-know-about-Etsy-s-Creative-Standards-policy); [TechCrunch, Jul 9, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/etsy-new-seller-policy-2024-generative-ai); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/ai-image-video/etsy-will-let-you-sell-ai-art-on-its-platform-but-theres-a-catch)
- Etsy reportedly removed its allowance for designs based on templated patterns on **June 10, 2025**. POD is allowed only if the partner is disclosed. — [Marmalead](https://blog.marmalead.com/etsy-print-on-demand-what-you-should-shouldnt-do/)
- **Redbubble — conflicting reports.** One blog says Redbubble added an AI disclosure checkbox in 2025 and limits new accounts to **5 AI-tagged uploads per day**, rising to 50 per day for high performers. Another analysis says Redbubble's Community and Content Guidelines don't mention AI at all. — [FelloAI](https://felloai.com/can-you-sell-ai-art/) vs. [AutoKeyworder](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/sell-ai-generated-art/)
- **TeePublic:** a vendor blog says TeePublic has **no AI disclosure field**, and an unofficial guide cites a 50-uploads/day limit. Neither is official. — [AutoKeyworder](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/autokeyworder-on-teepublic); [unofficial Notion guide](https://closed-pilot-dec.notion.site/Teepublic-d50459bab1e54b43906dc965a4ebaf36)
- **Society6:** moved to a curated, application-only marketplace around **March 2025** and closed many artist accounts. The stated aim was to cut low-quality and **AI-generated content**. An unconfirmed second purge was reported for Oct 2025. — [Wildlife Art Store](https://www.wildlifeartstore.com/sell-art-on-society6/); [Soil & Stars Substack](https://soilandstars.substack.com/p/im-closing-my-society6-art-shop); [EcommerceCEO](https://www.ecommerceceo.com/best-society6-alternatives/)
- **Crowdspring** (design-contest marketplace) bans AI-created designs entirely because they can't be protected by copyright or trademark. — [Crowdspring Help](https://www.crowdspring.com/help/rules-code-of-conduct-creatives/can-i-use-ai-generated-art-in-any-project/)
- **Amazon AI merch (2026):** secondary coverage says generation is subject to automated moderation that flags potential IP infringement, and that protected branding is routinely blocked. Commentators warn that AI outputs reproducing trademarked art could expose Merch on Demand to IP claims at scale. — [hyper.ai](https://hyper.ai/en/stories/d78d511bcce6621052827f6e57a35f46); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/amazon-lets-shoppers-design-custom-merch-via-alexa-for-shopping/)
- **Printify ChatGPT app:** terms disclose that user inputs are passed to Google Gemini image models, a privacy point to mirror in a Japanese service's terms. — [Printify ChatGPT App Terms](https://printify.com/chatgpt-app-terms-of-use/)
- **Threadless:** I found no published AI policy for Artist Shops or the marketplace. — (gap)

**Incidents and litigation**
- **Twitter art-stealing bots (Dec 2019):** artists suspected that bots scraped images from tweets replying "I want this on a shirt" and auto-listed them as shirts. Hannah Douken's bait image was auto-printed. Hans-Jürgen Eisenbeis (@Nirbion) baited the bots with an unlicensed Mickey Mouse image, and many sites quickly pulled those shirts. Commentators noted that POD platforms such as Moteefe hid seller identities. — [Waxy.org, Dec 2019](https://waxy.org/2019/12/how-artists-on-twitter-tricked-spammy-t-shirt-stores-into-admitting-their-automated-art-theft); [Fortune, Dec 6, 2019](https://fortune.com/2019/12/06/we-committed-copyright-infringement-and-want-to-be-sued-by-disney); [AV Club](https://avclub.com/artists-attempt-to-pit-art-stealing-twitter-bots-agains-1840223505); [Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/news/twitter-users-are-trying-to-take-down-art-stealing-bots-by-getting-disney-lawyers-involved-3962353)
- **Atari v. Redbubble:** filed 2018 in N.D. Cal., alleging Redbubble was "powered by a substantial quantity of counterfeit goods." The jury found no infringement, the Ninth Circuit affirmed for Redbubble, and rehearing was denied on **Oct 24, 2023**. — [Morrison Foerster](https://mofo.com/resources/news/231025-morrison-foerster-defeats-rehearing-of-trademark-win-for-redbubble); [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/tag/jury/); [Sports Litigation Alert](https://sportslitigationalert.com/?p=16963)
- **Brandy Melville v. Redbubble:** a jury found Redbubble liable on some claims, and the judge then cut the award by more than half. The Ninth Circuit later partly revived the issue (likelihood-of-confusion standard for one mark), opening the door to a potential injunction. — [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/trademark/1097852/update-redbubble-jury-award-reduced-by-more-than-half); [The Fashion Law](https://thefashionlaw.com/?p=92959); [IPWatchdog, Mar 2021](https://ipwatchdog.com/2021/03/09/litigation-threatening-burst-redbubble-courts-weigh-ip-implications-redbubbles-unique-online-marketplace/)
- **Artists v. Shein (filed July 2023):** designers Krista Perry, Larissa Martinez and Jay Baron allege that Shein's algorithmic trend system produces exact copies of their work. Judge Mark Scarsi (C.D. Cal.) **refused to dismiss the RICO claim**, which uses copyright infringement as a predicate act. I found no final judgment. — [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/court-keeps-rico-claim-against-shein-in-play-in-algorithmic-infringement-lawsuit/); [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/833373/artists-accuse-fashion-brand-shein-of-using-ai-to-steal-their-designs/)
- **Disney & Universal v. Midjourney (June 2025):** a 110-page complaint seeking up to **$150,000 per infringed work** and an injunction. Warner Bros. Discovery filed a similar suit in Sept 2025. The court suggested mediation in **Nov 2025**, and there has been no liability finding so far. — [Graphic Policy, Nov 14, 2025](https://graphicpolicy.com/2025/11/14/court-suggests-disney-v-midjourney-go-to-mediation/); [LA Mag](https://lamag.com/news/disney-and-universal-sue-ai-company-midjourney-for-copyright-infringement/); [SAN](https://san.com/cc/disney-universal-seek-150k-per-image-in-new-ai-lawsuit/)
- **AI flood claims:** a vendor guide claims AI designs make up about 12–15% of new Etsy and Redbubble listings, up from under 3% in 2023. This is unverified and comes from an AI-tool vendor. — [Cliprise](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/guides/getting-started/ai-print-on-demand-complete-guide-2026)
- An artist closing their Society6 and Redbubble shops cited "poor return margins," "newly introduced tier systems," account fees, and "rampant art theft and AI copying everything." — [Tumblr post](https://www.tumblr.com/rdjpwns)

### Inferences
- A Japanese service that generates designs *and* sells them to third parties combines the generator's risk (Midjourney-type claims) with the platform's risk (Redbubble-type claims). Practical safeguards follow from what rivals do:
  - prompt and output IP filtering (Amazon's approach),
  - AI disclosure labels (Etsy),
  - curation or approval before marketplace listing (Society6),
  - notice-and-takedown with seller identification (the lesson of the Moteefe bot incident).
- Because AI-only images may not be copyrightable (Crowdspring's stated rationale), user "ownership" of AI designs listed for royalties needs careful terms in the platform's ToS (e.g., a license grant rather than a copyright claim).

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve official AI policy text for Redbubble, TeePublic, Threadless, Zazzle or Merch by Amazon (seller side).
- I found no reliable primary data on AI-content share or takedown volumes on POD marketplaces.
- The current status of the Brandy Melville remand and the Shein case (2025–2026) is unconfirmed.

## Q5. Lessons (winners / failures / pivots) and market sizes

### Takeaway
Global POD is estimated at roughly **$8–11B in 2025**, with forecasts of 20–26% CAGR. Custom T-shirt printing is about **$5.2B (2024) → $9.8B (2030)** per Grand View Research. Estimates vary widely by vendor. Three trends stand out:
- **Consolidation and margin capture:** Printful+Printify merged, Articore returned to profit partly by raising artist fees, and Amazon tiered its royalties.
- **Shakeouts:** Spring's legacy platform is winding down, and Society6 purged accounts.
- **Distribution as the moat:** Amazon's free AI design inside Alexa, and Printify and Fourthwall inside ChatGPT.

Winners combine owned demand (Amazon traffic, creator audiences) with fulfillment scale. Pure marketplaces relying on long-tail uploaded art are shrinking.

### Cited Findings

**Market sizes (global POD)**
- **IMARC:** **$11.4B in 2025 → $61.8B by 2034** (~20% CAGR 2026–2034); North America leads. — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/print-on-demand-market)
- **The Business Research Company:** **$6.59B (2024) → $8.1B (2025)**, 23.0% CAGR; a revised edition gives $8.16B for 2025. — [GII / TBRC](https://www.gii.tw/report/tbrc1816813-print-on-demand-global-market-report.html); [GII / TBRC (earlier)](https://www.gii.tw/report/tbrc1717290-print-on-demand-global-market-report.html)
- **Technavio:** market to grow by **$32.1B over 2025–2030 at 25.8% CAGR**. — [GII / Technavio](https://www.giikorea.co.kr/report/infi2005610-global-print-on-demand-market.html)
- **Coherent Market Insights:** about **$11B (2026) → $58B (2033)**, 22% CAGR. — [GII / Coherent](https://www.gii.tw/report/coh1935390-print-on-demand-market-by-component-software.html)
- **Ken Research:** the U.S. POD market is **$3.46B in 2025**, with apparel the dominant segment. — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/us-print-on-demand-market.md)

**Market sizes (custom T-shirt printing)**
- **Grand View Research:** **$5.2B in 2024 → $9.8B by 2030** (11.5% CAGR, 2025–2030), with a **$6.3B estimate for 2026**. In 2024, Asia-Pacific held the largest share (**36.4%**), offline channels had 51% and commercial end-use 56%. The **artwork design** segment is growing fastest at **12.1% CAGR**. — [Grand View Research](https://grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/custom-t-shirt-printing-market)
- Graphic-design shirts made up more than 57% of global revenue in 2024 (Printful citing Grand View). — [Printful blog](https://printful.com/blog/t-shirt-industry-statistics)
- **Technavio** (Mar 2025): +$2.23B over 2024–2029 at 7.7% CAGR. — [Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/custom-t-shirt-printing-market-industry-analysis)
- **SkyQuest:** $4.17B (2024) → $9.59B (2033). — [GII / SkyQuest](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/sky1907605-custom-t-shirt-printing-market-size-share-growth.html)
- **Research and Markets:** $8.17B (2024) → $13.55B (2029), 10.8% CAGR. — [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5513511/custom-t-shirt-printing-market-2024-2028)

**Winners, failures and pivots**
- **Articore (Redbubble + TeePublic), FY26 (to June 30, 2026):**
  - Total revenue fell 6.9% to **A$408.5M**, and marketplace revenue fell 6.5% to **A$354.5M**.
  - Statutory net profit was **A$10.9M**, against an A$11.3M loss in FY25. EBIT was A$10.3M, its first full-year profit since listing outside the FY21 pandemic spike.
  - Gross margin hit a record **49.6%** (Redbubble **52.2%**), and opex fell to A$85.0M from A$91.3M.
  - TeePublic marketplace revenue rose 2.8% in constant currency, with gross profit up 10.9%.
  - Commentary attributes the recovery partly to **changes to artist fees**.
  - — [Smallcaps](https://smallcaps.com.au/article/articore-group-returns-to-profitability-in-fy26-as-gross-margin-hits-record); [Ragtrader](https://www.ragtrader.com.au/news/profits-go-green-for-redbubble-parent-articore); [Internet Retailing](https://internetretailing.com.au/articore-reports-revenue-decline-lifts-gross-profit/); [ASX announcement](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20251015/pdf/06qk4jql5jh5gj.pdf)
- **Printful + Printify merger (2024)** consolidated the two largest POD fulfillment platforms under one holding company with IPO ambitions. — [Printful newsroom](https://www.printful.com/ca/news/merged+company+leadership+team); [LSM](https://eng.lsm.lv/article/economy/business/08.01.2025-merger-of-the-year-as-printful-and-printify-become-fyul.a582814/)
- **Amazon (June 2026)** pairs free AI design with Prime fulfillment and, separately, ties Merch royalties to external traffic. It is described as a direct challenge to Redbubble, Bonfire, Spring and Fourthwall. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/amazon-now-lets-you-design-custom-merch-using-ai/); [Merch Titans](https://merchtitans.com/blog/amazon-merch-royalty-changes-june-2026)
- **Spring/Amaze:** shutting down its legacy platform, with about 40% opex cuts and a pivot to a $9.99/month subscription. Wikipedia reports a staged shutdown from Sept 15, 2026 (unconfirmed). — [Quiver Quant](https://www.quiverquant.com/news/Amaze%2BHoldings%2BProvides%2BUpdate%2Bon%2BProduct%2BRoadmap,%2BSubscription%2BRollout%2Band%2BPath%2Bto%2BProfitability); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teespring)
- **Society6:** curation pivot and account purge in 2025, with fixed markups. — [Wildlife Art Store](https://www.wildlifeartstore.com/sell-art-on-society6/); [EcommerceCEO](https://www.ecommerceceo.com/best-society6-alternatives/)
- **Spreadshirt:** a March 2024 blog reports that it introduced a custom-creation area full of free clip art and pre-designed tees, and that freelance designers saw lower profits as a result. This is one third-party perspective. — [search extract; Spreadshop blog cluster](https://www.spreadshop.com/blog/?p=13733)
- **Gelato:** reached unicorn status in 2021 through a network model (100+ partners, 32 countries) rather than owned factories. — [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/gelato-unicorn-240m)
- **Creator-storefront shift:** Articore's Dashery (some creators >$100k gross in year one) and Fourthwall's ChatGPT app point to growth in audience-led storefronts over anonymous marketplaces. — [Ragtrader](https://www.ragtrader.com.au/news/articore-to-double-3-2m-strategic-bet-in-fy26); [Fourthwall blog](https://fourthwall.com/blog/chatgpt-fourthwall-integration)

### Inferences
- **What differentiates winners:**
  1. Owned demand or traffic: Amazon, creator audiences, and ChatGPT as a new distribution surface.
  2. Fulfillment scale and network economics: Printful/Printify, Gelato.
  3. Pricing power over creators: Redbubble's fee tiers and Amazon's royalty tiers improved platform margins.
  4. Curation and trust as AI floods supply: Society6 curation, Etsy disclosure.
- **What failed or is fading:** pure long-tail marketplaces dependent on organic SEO (Redbubble's revenue decline) and thin-margin creator POD without a subscription or traffic moat (Spring's legacy platform).
- **Implications for the Japanese plan:**
  - Free AI design should be the funnel.
  - Revenue should come from the per-order margin plus a marketplace spread.
  - Royalties should be in the ~10% or fixed-amount range.
  - Royalties could be boosted when creators bring external traffic (Amazon-style).
  - IP filtering and curation must be built in from day one.
  - Using the same model family as Printify's ChatGPT app (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image / Imagen) is viable but not a differentiator.
- Grand View's report that Asia-Pacific holds the largest custom-T-shirt share (36.4%) suggests regional demand is healthy, although Japan-specific sizing is outside this scope.

### Gaps
- POD market-size figures disagree by vendor ($8.1B vs $11.4B for 2025) because definitions differ, and full reports are paywalled. Only publisher excerpts were used.
- I found no reliable data on the share of POD apparel orders that use AI-generated designs.
- Revenue and order figures for Printful/Printify (Fyul), Zazzle, Threadless, Spreadshirt, Fourthwall and Bonfire weren't gathered (private companies; quota exhausted).
- Real-world creator economics from Reddit were only partially captured (Redbubble fee complaints). I found no Reddit threads on the June 2026 Amazon royalty change.
